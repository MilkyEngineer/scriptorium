# S3 — Paper Pro Move `.rm` rendering with rmscene

**Status:** passed. **Date:** 2026-10-17. **Fork tag:** `scriptorium-s3` on `MilkyEngineer/remarkable-mcp` (renderer changes on top of `scriptorium-s0`). **Fixture:** `tests/fixtures/move-test-notebook/` (purpose-written, synthetic ids, scrubbed author id).

## Question

Does rmscene parse what the Move writes, and can the fork's renderer (rmscene → SVG → resvg) reproduce the pages, including geometry, colours, highlighter blending, eraser, layers, scrolled pages and annotated PDFs?

## Fixture

A 7-page notebook written on the Move for this purpose plus a one-page annotated PDF (the standard "This is a test PDF document" sample), both exported through the cloud with S0's client and committed under xochitl layout with document, page and author UUIDs replaced by fixed synthetic ones. Web-app screenshots of every page are the references (`references/`). Pages: default fineliner; ballpoint plus four highlighter colours; paintbrush, marker, pencil, calligraphy, mechanical pencil, shader; shapes; highlighter over ink plus eraser; two layers; a scrolled page written at the far left and right edges. No typed text page (not covered; see below).

## Findings

**Parsing.** All eight `.rm` files are `version=6`. rmscene 0.8.0 reads every block; the only unread data is 111 bytes of `extra_data` on each `SceneInfo` block, which rmscene logs as "Some data has not been read". Decoded by hand, it holds floats: the page's paper size again (820.29 × 1458.3 for notebooks) and, for the PDF page, a rectangle (−1717, −468, 3434 × 3433) and (1404, 1872), consistent with a scrollable canvas around the page. No unknown block types, no unknown pen or colour ids. Pens seen: `FINELINER_2`, `BALLPOINT_2`, `HIGHLIGHTER_2`, `PAINTBRUSH_2`, `MARKER_2`, `PENCIL_2`, `CALIGRAPHY`, `MECHANICAL_PENCIL_2`, `SHADER`. Erased strokes are already absent from the parsed scene (page 5 renders exactly as the device shows it), and the two-layer page carries two `SceneTreeBlock`s composited in order.

**Geometry (notebooks).** `SceneInfo.paper_size` is (820, 1458). Stroke coordinates are centre-origin in x and top-origin in y. Measured against the references on six pages, the mapping is linear with x = 0 at the horizontal centre and y = 0 at the top (within 1 %), so the unit is the same device-independent unit earlier devices use (≈ 226 per inch) and the Move's 954 × 1696 panel is 954/820 = 1.163 px per unit. The ink on the scrolled page spans x ∈ [−693, 698] and y up to 3740, i.e. well outside the 820 × 1458 rectangle: the Move's editor scrolls horizontally as well as vertically (the operator wrote the far-left and far-right labels by panning, not zooming), so **the paper size is a viewport, not a bound**, and the writable canvas is at least ~1400 units wide, plausibly the full Paper Pro page. The archive render is the union of the paper rectangle and the ink bounding box. `.content` records `verticalScroll` and `scrollTime` per page but nothing about horizontal extent.

**Geometry (annotated PDFs).** The `.rm` for the PDF page reports paper 1404 × 1872, which is misleading. Measured against the reference using the PDF's own text position (pypdfium2 char boxes) and the ink: the page is laid out at **1 pt = 226/72 = 3.139 units**, origin at the page's top centre, so a Letter page is 1921 × 2486 units (measured 1921 × ~2990 including the canvas below the page). Compositing the pypdfium2 render at that scale under the ink reproduces the reference exactly. `cPages.pages[].redir` gives the PDF page index (0 here); `pagedata` is `Blank`.

**Colours.** Paper Pro / Move strokes carry `color_rgba` for colours that have no palette id: blue highlighter (190, 234, 254), orange highlighter (255, 195, 140), shader (116, 210, 232, α 102). The other colours use palette ids whose rendered values, sampled from the web app, differ from the fork's legacy map: pink highlighter #EC61CA, green highlighter #BBF874, marker red #B33E39, cyan #74D2E8. Highlighter ink is drawn with multiply blending at roughly 0.9 opacity, so pen strokes stay visible through it. Paintbrush, pencil and shader have texture on the device that a solid stroke does not reproduce; the colour and placement match, the texture does not.

**Reproducing the goldens (recorded 2026-10-18 while writing `library/render.py`).** Notebook pages: `render_rm_file_extent_png(path, "#FFFFFF", 954/820 × scale, margin=20)`; the 20-unit margin around the ink box is required, pages 03 and 07 come out a different size without it. The PDF page: pypdfium2 renders at 226/72 × 954/820 px per point, Lanczos-resized to the page rectangle's exact pixel size; the ink is rasterised by resvg with viewBox and width/height both in units (resvg fits the width, so the layer is cropped or padded top-left), highlighter/shader strokes multiplied with the page and the rest alpha-composited over it. All eight goldens match byte for byte with Pillow's default PNG encoder on macOS. On Linux (CI, and a `python:3.12-slim` container) the PNG bytes differ but the seven notebook pages are pixel-identical; the PDF page differs in 0.41% of its pixels, all inside the two lines of PDF text, because the fixture PDF does not embed Arial and Times New Roman and PDFium substitutes its built-in fonts where the platform has none. The ink is pixel-identical on both platforms, so the test requires exact ink and allows up to 1% of page pixels to differ.

**Changes made to the fork (`scriptorium-s3`).** `_v6_paths_from_blocks` honours `color_rgba`, uses a separate highlighter palette, corrects red and cyan, and blends highlighters and the shader with multiply. New `render_rm_file_extent_png(path, background, scale, margin)` returns the PNG and the rendered viewBox in units (the page rectangle grown to the ink). The existing full-page function is untouched for upstream's write tool. 17 new tests; upstream suite unchanged (480 passed).

**Performance.** 0.12–0.22 s per page through resvg at 1× on an M-series laptop, no PyMuPDF present.

## Decisions

- Scriptorium's `render.py` uses the fork's `render_rm_file_extent_png` at 1.163 px per unit for notebooks; for PDF pages it builds the canvas itself: PDF page via pypdfium2 at 226/72 units per point at top centre, ink overlaid, union with the ink extent.
- Templates are not rendered (not in the document store); references show the ruled template, goldens are white.
- Architecture §8.3 updated with the verified numbers; the "954 × 1696 to confirm" wording is replaced.
- Follow-ups: add a typed-text page and a landscape page to the fixture when convenient; consider upstreaming the colour patch.
